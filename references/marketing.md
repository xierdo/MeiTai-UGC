# Marketing brief (internal by default)

Adapted in our own wording from the four-sales framework and two reviewed skills. Treat it as a creative planning tool, not proof of performance.

## Compact brief
For each concept answer these eight questions in a line each, mentally for simple requests:
1. Audience: one consumer segment, not just age/gender. What behavior or context matters?
2. Pain/desire: what concrete inconvenience or aspiration matters now?
3. Awareness: unfamiliar with solutions, knows the problem, knows solutions, knows product, or nearly ready to purchase? This is a hypothesis unless data supports it.
4. Concept/format: why would this particular audience watch this story or test?
5. Dual hook: what can be seen immediately? What exact Thai line adds context rather than repeating the visual?
6. Care logic: what verified, understandable action addresses the problem? No invented mechanisms, ingredient penetration, exclusivity or medical claims.
7. Product value: which documented feature, use or benefit is relevant? Prefer one idea clearly expressed.
8. Action: what realistic next step follows (save, ask for link, consider bundle, shop)? No fake urgency, pricing, scarcity, certification, guarantees or endorsements.

## Four persuasive jobs
- Content: topic, format, concept, visual hook, spoken hook.
- Hope: recognize the situation and give a credible path forward. If supported, explain problem / desired care function / product's role. Avoid claiming alternatives fail or only this product can help.
- Value: connect the product's verified use and benefit to the person's goal. Explain why the two products are complementary when relevant. Use actual proof only; otherwise portray an illustration, not evidence.
- Action: a natural next step. Offers or urgency require real, current evidence. Avoid forcing CTA into a casual conversation that ends naturally.

## Choose by audience and format
- Broad discovery: curiosity or situation first, then the relevant benefit or care logic.
- Problem-aware: recognizable daily inconvenience, practical routine, product fit.
- Solution-aware: verified differences and usage; avoid unsupported competitor comparisons.
- Product-aware: product scale, texture, routine, documented value or offer.
- Ready to buy: actual bundle/price/offer, if provided, or simple shopping action.
Duration alone does not determine awareness. A 10s clip can be a cold hook; a 28s dialogue can still serve a product-aware audience.

## Reference-ad reuse
Obtain the actual reference or transcript when available. Label mini-blocks by function, with time span, spoken intent, visible action and transition. Rebuild from the new product brief. Reuse timing/structure, not third-party wording, identity, footage, or fabricated testimonials. If unable to access the reference, disclose that and work from the user's description only.
